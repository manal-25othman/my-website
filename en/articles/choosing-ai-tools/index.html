How to choose an AI tool that actually fits your work

A practical four-step framework for evaluating AI tools before letting them into your daily workflow — past the marketing noise.

Every week a new AI tool promises to double your productivity. After months of testing you arrive at the same conclusion: most tools are excellent in the demo, and very few survive past the first week in a real workflow.

The problem is not the tools. It is how we pick them. We choose based on what a tool can do, rather than what we actually need done.

Start from the task, not the tool

Before opening any website, write down the task that genuinely consumes your time this week. Not "improve productivity" — something specific: summarising a ten-page weekly report, or drafting repetitive email replies.

A specific task gives you a clear test: either the tool did it, or it did not.

The four criteria

I put every tool through four questions:

  1. Does it save measurable time? Compare before and after on the same task, not on an idealised one.
  2. How good is the raw output? If you rewrite the result entirely, you saved nothing.
  3. Where does your data go? Especially if your work touches sensitive information.
  4. What does leaving cost? A tool that locks your data inside it costs more than its subscription.

A good tool disappears into your work. A bad one becomes the work.

Test for two weeks, then decide

Give the tool two weeks on one real task, not a staged demo. Record three things only: what it completed, what you fixed by hand, and whether you returned to it without a reminder.

If you are not reaching for it by week three, it is not your tool — however good it is.

The takeaway

AI does not compensate for an unclear process. Automating chaos produces faster chaos. Fix the task first, then pick the tool that serves it.
